# Shadow filtering

Filtered, softer shadows for units, structures and the ground they stand on, a larger shadow map, and optional contact hardening. Engine only: the game's data files are not changed. The setting is **Options, Video, Shadow Filtering** (render feature `shadow_filter`, console variable `ren_ShadowFilter`, levels Off / Low / Medium / High / Ultra); it applies at once, no restart. Not to be confused with FAF's own Shadow Fidelity (`shadow_quality`) and Shadow Resolution (`shadow_resolution`, `ren_ShadowSize`) options, which stay as they are.

It sits on top of the engine's own shadow work: cascades (`ren_ShadowCascades`), the stable fit, caster culling and tile reuse (Direct3D 12 and Vulkan), terrain depth in the shadow map, contact shadows and terrain shadows (`AmbientOcclusionRenderer`; the terrain shadows are off by default since 2026-11-13, `ren_TerrainShadows`), and ray-traced shadows. With the filter Off nothing of those changes (the shader text is not even patched).

## How the engine makes shadows

All of it is in `src/moho/render/Shadow.cpp` (one `Shadow` per viewport) and the shipped `mesh.fx` / `terrain.fx`.

1. `PrepareLightCamera` fits an orthographic light volume to the part of the view frustum within `ren_ShadowCoeff * zoom` of the camera and above the terrain. On Direct3D 12 and Vulkan the shadow texture is an atlas of up to four tiles of that light space (`ren_ShadowCascades`, `gpg/gal/effects/ShadowCascades.h`): tile 0 is the whole-view map, the others are finer boxes near the camera.
2. `RenderShadowMap` draws the terrain's depth and then every mesh into a colour target (16-bit R and G): **R = light-space depth** of the nearest thing to the light (cleared to 1), **G = 0** wherever that is a mesh, 1 on the terrain and the cleared texels. **The shipped terrain depth is wrong:** `TerrainDepthVS` of `terrain.fx` multiplies the whole position, `w` included, by `HeightScale` (1/128) and `TerrainDepthPS` writes the interpolated clip-space `z`, which is 1/128 of the depth (the rasterised depth, z / w, is right, which is why the terrain occludes the meshes correctly; only the value written is small). So R reads about 0 on the terrain (measured on Direct3D 12). The shipped lookups never use it: the ground reads G, the meshes' 5-tap lookup happens to shade a mesh wherever the terrain is nearest, whatever its depth.
3. With `ren_ShadowBlur` two separable 5-tap passes blur G into a second pair of targets.
4. **Meshes** (`ComputeShadowPCF`) sample the map with five bilinear taps and compare each with the receiver's depth; **the ground and the decals** (`ComputeShadow` and six more sites in `terrain.fx`) read the blurred G with one bilinear tap.
5. The effect layer gives every lookup of the shadow sampler a remap to the finest tile that holds the point (`faf_shadow_sampler`, `ModernHlsl.cpp`).

## What this adds

| Level | Shadow map (at least) | Lookup, meshes and ground | Blur passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off | FAF's `ren_ShadowSize` (1024) | shipped | on |
| Low | 1024 | 8 taps, rotated disk, radius 1.5 texels | not run |
| Medium | 2048 | 12 taps, radius 2 texels | not run |
| High | 2048 | blocker search (8 taps) + 16 taps, radius 1.5 to 6 texels (contact hardening) | not run |
| Ultra | 4096 | blocker search (8 taps) + 24 taps, radius 2 to 10 texels | not run |

`ren_ShadowSize` (FAF's Shadow Resolution slider) can still ask for a larger map than the level's; Off leaves it exactly as it was, and the user's value is never overwritten by a level.

The lookup (`ShadowFilterLookup`, generated by `src/moho/render/shadow/ShadowEffectPatch.cpp`) is shared by `mesh.fx` and `terrain.fx`:

- **Depth comparison on the ground too.** With the filter on, the ground reads the raw map and compares its depth with the depth of everything in it: meshes and, since the terrain depth is written correctly (the filter's patch of `TerrainDepthPS` writes `z / w`), the terrain itself. A unit only shadows what is behind it, as seen from the sun, and a hill shades the ground behind it. The two blur targets are not allocated any more, which pays for the larger map.
- **Cascade-aware.** The lookup finds the tile that holds its centre once (`tex2Dtile`, `tex2Dtilesize`, `tex2Dtileload` of the effect layer: Direct3D 12 and Vulkan; the same names exist for plain textures, where the tile is the identity) and takes every tap in the texels of that tile, as offsets in the atlas's own coordinates with a point snap to the atlas's texel centres. A finer tile is read at its own texel size; the blocker search and the penumbra scale convert between tile texels and whole-view coordinates. A point no tile holds is lit, as the shipped lookup has it. The cascade margin (`CascadeMarginTexels`) and the caster-cull margin are at least the kernel's reach plus one texel (up to 11 at Ultra), so no tap leaves its tile. A level's map size is the *tile* size when the map is an atlas, at most 2048 (`kAtlasTileLimit`) unless `ren_ShadowSize` asks for more: Ultra would be an 8192 x 8192 atlas twice.
- **Rotated Vogel disk.** The taps sit on an equal-area golden-angle spiral; the disk is rotated per shadow-map texel by interleaved gradient noise. The noise is fixed to the shadow map (the world), so nothing shimmers when the camera moves. Tap weights fall off towards the rim.
- **Receiver-plane bias.** The slope of the receiver's depth over the shadow map is derived from the screen-space derivatives of the shadow coordinate (`ddx`/`ddy`), and every tap compares against the receiver depth moved along its plane. A wide kernel on a slope no longer shadows its own surface, so the constant bias is halved (`ren_ShadowFilterBias`, default 0.5; `ren_ShadowFilterSlope`, default 1, scales the plane term). Checked at a very close view: bias 0.25, 0.5, 1 and slope 0 give the same picture on the commander and the ground.
- **The ground's own bias is small, the terrain's margin is separate.** A ground pixel is shaded on the same triangles the map's terrain was drawn from, so its depth is the map's up to the plane over the kernel (the receiver-plane term covers it) and the 0.0005 the vertex shaders' epsilon leaves. A constant bias as large as a mesh's would leave a strip of sun between a unit's base and its shadow (seen 2026-10-28 at a 3x crop of a structure's foot, D3D12), so the ground keeps `0.0003 * ren_ShadowFilterBias` on top of that epsilon. The terrain *as a caster* is moved `ren_ShadowFilterTerrainBias` (1 world unit) away from the light: the map holds the terrain's mesh seen from the light, and where the ground is curved it differs from the shaded surface (the blocks the margin removes are about a terrain cell wide, so the cause is read as the mesh's triangles, not measured). Without the margin curved slopes showed blocks in shadow of themselves (seen on `adaptive_acid_lakes` at Medium, D3D12); 0.5 world unit already removes them, 1 is the default. Meshes as casters keep the small bias.
- **Contact hardening (High, Ultra).** A first pass over 8 taps within the largest radius averages the depth of the casters in front of the receiver. The kernel radius is `(receiver depth - blocker depth) * penumbra scale`, clamped between the level's smallest and largest radius: a shadow is sharp where the object touches the ground and softer further away. The penumbra scale is derived from the light volume (`Shadow::PrepareLightCamera` reports its width and depth range) and the light's angular size (tan 0.04). No blocker in the search disk returns "lit" at once, which also makes most of the screen cheaper.
- **Hardware comparison samplers are not used.** The shadow map is a colour target the depth pass writes (depth in R together with the G mask), not a depth texture, so `SampleCmp` / a Vulkan comparison sampler would need a different depth pass. The comparison is done in the shader on single-texel fetches (`tex2Dlod`), which is the same on every API. On the ground effect `terrain.fx` has no sampler to spare (a pass of it already uses all 16 that shader model 4.1 allows), so its taps read the shipped bilinear `ShadowSampler` at texel centres, which returns exactly that texel.
- **Hills shade the ground through this lookup,** within the light volume (a hill beyond the volume does not shade: the terrain the map pass draws is the view's own tessellation, see "Hills outside the view" below; the terrain shadows of `ren_Ao`'s compute pass bake a march over the height field beyond the map's reach, and Dynamic Lights' light field takes the bake's place while the terrain takes its march: `lights::TerrainSunShadowActive()`, `ren_DynLightsTerrainSunShadow`, off by default, [Ambient occlusion](AMBIENT_OCCLUSION.md)). The terrain depth fix is part of the filter's effect patch: with the filter Off the shipped `terrain.fx` is used untouched.
- **Yields to ray-traced shadows.** While `ren_RayTracedShadows` traces (`RayScene::Active()`), `Active()` is false: the shipped lookups, the blur and the shipped map size are used, which the ray queries rewrite and complete. Switching rays off brings the filter back identically (checked).

## Shader side: how it coexists with the dynamic lights

Both features patch `mesh.fx` and `terrain.fx` as the text is read (`CD3DEffect::InitEffectFromFile`). They touch different places (this patch: the shadow lookup functions and the `tex2D(ShadowSampler, ...).g` sites; the lights: the vertex shaders, the PBR helper area and the technique list) and share one patch point instead of fighting over the text:

1. `CD3DEffect::InitEffectFromFile` (`CD3DEffectTechnique.cpp`) builds the text of an effect. It runs the shadow patch first and hands its output to the lights patch (`LightEffectPatch`), which only needs its own anchors (and knows the lookups the shadow patch leaves: `ShadowLookup(`). A source with CRLF line ends is normalised to LF by the shadow patch (the lights patch's anchors are LF).
2. Each patch that was applied adds a tag to the cache name (`mesh.dynlights.shadowf.<version>`), so every combination compiles to its own cached effect.
3. A patch that does not recognise the source (a FAF update moved something) leaves the text as it was and logs why; the effect then compiles without it (`ShadowFilter: mesh not patched: ...`). If the filtered effect does not compile on a device (Direct3D 11 compiles at load), the effect is built again without the filter and with the lights patch alone, and the filter stays off (`Active()` needs both effects patched).
4. The host test (`tests/shadow/ShadowEffectPatchTests.cpp`, `FAF_STOCK_FX_DIR`) applies both patches in both orders and checks the result.

The patch is applied on the backends that take the Direct3D 10 effect dialect (Direct3D 11, 12, Vulkan) only while the level is not Off when the effects load (`shadowfilter::EffectPatchWanted`): with the filter Off the shipped effects are compiled and cached untouched. Switching it on in a running game rebuilds mesh.fx and terrain.fx once, together with the lights' effects when both are switched on (`lights::ApplyLiveLevel`: 91 ms on Direct3D 12, on Direct3D 11 12.6 s compiled on worker threads while the game goes on, then 49 ms; measured). Between levels the shader parameter (`shadowFilter.x` / `ShadowFilter.x` = tap count; 0 runs the shipped code) switches live. One frame costs two `float4` parameter writes per effect.

Parameters of the patched text (all set by `ShadowFilter.cpp` every frame): `float4 shadowFilter, shadowFilter2` in `mesh.fx`, `ShadowFilter, ShadowFilter2` in `terrain.fx`. `x` taps (0 = shipped), `y` radius in texels, `z` largest radius, `w` penumbra scale; `filter2.x` bias scale, `filter2.y` plane bias scale.

## Per API

| API | Shadow Filtering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Direct3D 12 | Off, Low, Medium, High, Ultra | tile-aware on the cascade atlas |
| Vulkan | same | same |
| Direct3D 11 | same | no cascades there: the texture is the map |
| Direct3D 9 | not offered | the shipped ps_2_a techniques have too few constant registers |
| Direct3D 10 | not offered | the renderer does not start |

Default: **Off** on every API; Medium is the level to try first.

## Verified (2026-11-12, RX 7900 XTX, windowed 1600x900, the staged static scene (structures of four factions and idle units, `AB_SCENE static` of the test harness) on `adaptive_acid_lakes`, game paused, profile with every render feature Off)

- Levels Off to Ultra, same paused frame, crops of a unit's shadow edge on Direct3D 12, Vulkan and Direct3D 11: the filtered levels draw a sharper edge and, from High, a penumbra that widens with the distance to the caster; the ground is lit where nothing shades it (the first version, which compared the ground with the terrain's own R, drew the whole ground black). Direct3D 12 and Vulkan agree (mean difference to Off 0.88 to 0.95 levels in the crop, 3.6 to 3.9 % of the pixels above 8 levels on both).
- Filter Off against the same scene on the build of the base branch (`rb/lights`, which carries upstream's cascades, stable fit, caster culling and tile reuse): Vulkan mean absolute difference 0.001 to 0.035 levels, at most 0.09 % of the pixels above 8 levels; Direct3D 12 0.07 to 0.23, which is animated effects and the clock of the interface, not shadows.
- Live switch on: Direct3D 12 91 ms, Direct3D 11 12.6 s in the background then 49 ms. Ray-traced shadows on (Direct3D 12 and Vulkan, 635 instances, no hang on this card): the filter yields, and with rays off again it is back and the frame identical to before (difference 0).
- The Options entry from Lua (`GetOptionsData()`): present on all three APIs, default Off.

## Open points of the terrain shadows (2026-11-12, `fix/left`, RX 7900 XTX, `adaptive_acid_lakes`, game paused, test sun `ren_SunTestElevation` / `ren_SunTestAzimuth`)

**Hills outside the view (not fixed, cause found).** Extending the light camera's near plane so that it holds the highest terrain of the map that can still shade the volume (the nearest corner of the light volume moved back by `(highest terrain - corner height) / sin(elevation)` along the light, capped at 1000 units; a pure function with a host test) changed nothing: at a sun elevation of 5 degrees the log showed the near plane moved 243.9 units back (map height 15 to 36.5, volume corner heights from about 15) and Medium frames with the extension on and off were identical (mean difference 0.01 levels, 0.00 % of the pixels above 12; elevations 5, 10, 17 and 40, Direct3D 12). The terrain in the shadow map is not clipped by the near plane: `HighFidelityTerrain::DrawTerrainDepth` draws the one prebuilt terrain batch (`DrawTriangles`), which `UpdateRenderContext` tessellates from the view camera (`mTesselator->Rebuild(mCamera, ...)`), so a hill that is not in the view is not in the batch and cannot enter the map however the light volume is fitted. A real fix needs a second terrain batch built for the light (tessellated for the light camera, which the tessellator takes only from a camera and for a frame's one LOD) or the height-field march that the terrain shadows of `ren_Ao` / the light field already do beyond the map's reach; both cost more than a margin, so the extension was dropped. What a user sees: at a low sun the shadow of a hill that stands outside the view ends at the view's edge (with ray-traced shadows, the reference, it continues; Medium against rays on the view at the ridge, sun 5 / 10 / 17 / 40 degrees: rays shade more of the ground near the top of the view, the rest agrees, mean difference 1.34 / 0.45 / 0.10 / 0.04 levels). The shadow distance (`ren_ShadowCoeff * zoom`) also lights ground beyond it by design.

**Low sun and steep slopes (verified).** The same ridge (slope up to 2, a 63 degree face; map heights 15 to 36) at 30 units of camera zoom, Medium (1024 map) and Ultra, sun 5, 10, 17 and 40 degrees, crops of the terminator on the slope on Direct3D 11, 12 and Vulkan: no self-shadow speckle, no light leaking through the ridge, no blocks, at any elevation or API; Direct3D 12 and Vulkan agree to 0.15 levels over the 1400 x 650 view at every elevation, Direct3D 11 differs by 2.7 to 4.7 levels (no cascades there, a different map). At 5 degrees the whole lee slope is in shadow, as it should be.

**The blocks (measured, still not explained).** With `ren_ShadowFilterTerrainBias 0` the ridge's lit slope shows a speckled patch of self-shadow (Medium, zoom 30, sun 40 and 17 degrees, Direct3D 12): 0.33 / 0.29 levels and 0.6 / 0.4 % of the pixels above 16 against the default bias 1. A map four times as fine (`ren_ShadowSize` 4096 against 1024, same view) draws the same patch in the same place (0.11 / 0.13 levels, 0.02 / 0.01 % of the pixels differ), so it is not the map's resolution, its quantisation or the kernel; and the earlier reading that the map pass draws a different terrain mesh than the one shaded is wrong as well, both passes draw the same prebuilt batch. What remains is the two vertex shaders' positions of the same triangles (`TerrainDepthVS` against the shading pass's) and the receiver's interpolated depth; telling them apart needs a debug output of the receiver's depth against the map's at one texel, which was not built. The margin (`ren_ShadowFilterTerrainBias`, 1 unit) removes the patch and stays.

## Not verified

Direct3D 9 and 10; several viewports; other maps, steep slopes; a hill outside the view (does not shade, see below); the exact cause of the blocks the terrain margin removes (what it is not is measured, see below); replays; a fresh profile's stored level applied at start-up; the water's sun shadow (the water-reflection patch of the water work reads the shadow target through a sampler of its own, outside the cascade remap: it needs the same tile helpers when it is ported); the cost table of the earlier branch (4K) was not re-measured on this base.
